# Landslide Safety — Sample Reference Document

> Sample reference material prepared for the HillSense AI demonstration. Not an official government publication.

Landslides are among the deadliest hazards in the Himalayan belt, especially during the monsoon (July–September) when prolonged rainfall saturates slopes.

## Warning signs of an imminent landslide

- New cracks or unusual bulges appearing on slopes or roads
- Tilting trees, fences, or poles; doors and windows sticking in their frames
- Rumbling or popping sounds from the hillside
- Sudden muddy seepage or springs appearing on a slope
- Small rock or debris falls preceding larger movement

## Immediate actions during a landslide

- Move uphill or to higher stable ground, away from the slide path
- Warn people below the slope — debris travels far downhill
- Do not return to a building under a slope until it is declared safe
- Listen to local alerts; keep away from river channels carrying slide debris

## After the slide

- Stay off the debris mass; secondary slides are common after the first movement
- Report blocked roads and stranded people to the local administration
- Check for injured persons only when the slope is visibly stable
- Photograph damage for assessment and insurance records

## Prevention notes for settlements

- Avoid construction on cut slopes or at the base of steep hills
- Maintain surface drains so water does not soak into slopes
- Retain natural vegetation; deep roots bind slope material
