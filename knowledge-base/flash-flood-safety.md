# Flash Flood Safety — Sample Reference Document

> Sample reference material prepared for the HillSense AI demonstration. Not an official government publication.

Flash floods in mountain regions develop within minutes to hours of intense rain or cloudbursts, often far from where the rain actually falls. A dry nala can become a violent torrent with little local warning.

## Warning signs

- Rapidly rising or suddenly muddy water in rivers and streams
- A low rumble or deep growl coming from upstream
- Debris, twigs, or fresh silt suddenly floating past
- Intense rainfall over the hills upstream of your position
- A sudden clean smell of earth followed by a wall of water sound

## Immediate actions

- Move to high ground immediately — vertically is safest, not along the river
- Never try to outrun a flood along the valley floor
- Abandon vehicles and move up the valley wall on foot
- Warn camps, dhabas, and homestays close to the river

## Rules that save lives

- 15 cm of fast-moving water can knock an adult down; 60 cm can float a vehicle
- Never cross a flooded nala, causeway, or bridge approach
- Do not camp within the flood margin of any mountain river during rain
- After the water recedes, expect unstable banks and more surges upstream

## For tourists

- Ask locals about the river's behaviour before camping or parking near it
- Keep a charged phone and share your itinerary with your hotel
- During rain, treat every riverbank as temporary ground, not camp ground
