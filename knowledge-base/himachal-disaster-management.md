# Himachal Disaster Management — Sample Reference Document

> Sample reference material prepared for the HillSense AI demonstration. Not an official government publication.

Himachal Pradesh is among India's most hazard-exposed states: landslides and flash floods dominate the monsoon, avalanches affect higher reaches in winter, forest fires peak in dry summer, and the state lies in a high seismic zone.

## Hazard profile

- **Landslides:** concentrated along major national and state highways and in cut-slope settlements; monsoon is peak season
- **Flash floods:** cloudburst-driven surges in nalas and rivers; July–September
- **Rockfall:** active along steep road cuts, especially after rain and freeze–thaw
- **Forest fires:** dry summer months; pine forests are most flammable
- **Avalanches:** high passes and upper valleys in winter
- **Earthquakes:** the state falls in a high seismic zone — masonry buildings are vulnerable

## Response structure (how local response typically works)

- District administration (Deputy Commissioner) coordinates disaster response
- Police, fire services, and SDRF carry out search, rescue, and clearance
- PWD and BRO restore road connectivity
- Health services handle casualties; hospitals activate mass-casualty plans
- Village disaster management committees are the first local responders

## Information flow for citizen reports

- Emergency calls go to 112, the national emergency number
- 1077 is the state disaster helpline
- Citizens' first reports often alert authorities before formal detection — early, accurate reporting with location and photo saves response time
- Local radio and administration advisories are the primary public alert channels

## Monsoon-season priorities

- Keep drains and culverts clear
- Avoid travel on unstable slopes during active rain
- Report slope cracks and bulges early, before they fail
